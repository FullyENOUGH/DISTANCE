\section{Ecosystem and Crossed Distance: The Massive Disconnection Born of Connection}
\textbf{The Illusion of the Food Chain and the Momentum Mixer} \\
We have long understood the ecosystem as a pyramid-shaped 'food chain'. We picture grass, the herbivores that eat the grass, and the carnivores that rule above them in a hierarchical, unidirectional structure of survival. In this outdated perspective, the ecosystem is depicted as a bloody battlefield where the cruel law of the jungle and survival of the fittest prevail.
However, applying the chilling lens of DISTANCE reveals the ecosystem's true nature not as a linear chain, but as an infinite 'Network'. A blade of grass is connected to the sun, the rain, the soil, and the microbes. The herbivore connects to the grass, the predator connects to the herbivore, and the dead predator connects back to the soil through decomposers. There is no beginning and no end in the ecosystem. It is simply a massive 'Momentum Mixer' where countless momentum islands are tightly intertwined to continuously blend and scatter the universe's energy. \\
\textbf{Predation: The Fierce Cosmic Connection} \\
From this magnificent network's perspective, the biological meaning of 'Predation' is completely overturned. A lion hunting a zebra is not a simple cruel drama of the strong unilaterally exploiting and eliminating the weak. On a cosmic scale, predation is an act of fierce 'Connection' where two different momentum structures collide to mix energy.
The lion cannot exist without the zebra, and the zebra could not exist in its current agile and sensitive structure without the powerful external momentum of the lion. Outwardly, they seem to be fighting and shedding blood, but in reality, they are sharply forging each other's structures over thousands of generations. The lion evolves to hunt more precisely, and the zebra evolves to escape faster. Ultimately, predation is not a simple extinction, but an overarching cosmic furnace forging more complex and highly advanced 'new structures (new distances)' within the process of momentum resolution. \\
\textbf{The Paradox of Crossed Distance: Connection Births Disconnection} \\
Life's momentum resolution, however, does not stop at the 'consumption' of predation. Because every entity is finite and will eventually stop its flow, life devised an unprecedented mechanism to sustain this massive energy-mixing network: 'Reproduction' and the 'Differentiation of Sexes'.
Early single-celled organisms simply cloned themselves, but the infinite repetition of the exact same structure had clear limits in exploring new possibilities. Thus, life abandoned efficient simple replication and chose the differentiation of sexes to create new structures by colliding two different islands. Through the lens of DISTANCE, reproduction is absolutely not a teleological act to preserve the species or increase numbers. It is the 'ultimate act of connection', fiercely combining two different momentum islands to explore entirely new possibilities and mix energy that had never existed in the universe before.
At this exact point, the most bizarre and majestic physical paradox of life is exposed. Both predation and mating are fierce attempts to 'connect' and mix momentum. Countless life forms constantly struggle to approach, mix, and combine with each other to resolve the universe's energy gaps. But what happens when these fierce attempts to get closer accumulate over tens of thousands of years? 
Surprisingly, the repetition of connection that so desperately tried to mix energy eventually tears the populations apart into completely different species that can never mix again. A group that once mated and mixed energy freely repeats continuous connection and structure exploration until they are perfectly separated genetically, creating a massive chasm that can never be crossed.
This is the cosmic paradox of 'Crossed Distance' forged by evolution. The accumulated effort to get closer creates an uncrossable 'massive disconnection'. Connection births disconnection, and closeness births distance. Life constantly tries to flatten the universe by mixing momentum, but as a result of that attempt, it endlessly scatters new species (new distances) across cosmic space.
The millions of species existing in the universe are not a randomly generated, colorful zoo. They are the 'infinite flames of disconnection (speciation)' ignited by the universe, using the fierce 'connection' of predation and reproduction as fuel to mix its energy to the extreme.
