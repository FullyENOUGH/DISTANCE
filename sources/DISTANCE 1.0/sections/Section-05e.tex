\section{The Continuum of Life and Death: The Spectrum of Dynamicity}
\textbf{The Illusion of the Binary Boundary} \\
We are deeply accustomed to strictly dividing the world into 'the living' and 'the dead'. We firmly believe that rocks and rivers are dead, inanimate objects, while trees, fish, and humans are living beings. Life has been praised as a special and noble miracle operating on completely different principles from ordinary inorganic matter. Thus, we set life and death as a binary opposition, believing an uncrossable boundary exists between them. However, applying the chilling lens of DISTANCE and viewing the entire universe as a single massive flow of momentum resolution exposes this solid dichotomy as a perfect illusion.
If we clear away the desktop of spacetime, the actual hardware of the universe is scattered with countless 'Energy Islands'. A star is a massive island, a planet is an island, and a rock, the atmosphere, and a human cell are also energy islands holding their own momentum. The true nature of the universe is not the dramatic magic of creation and extinction, but a permanent 'Gathering and Scattering' (Ihapjipsan) where these countless energy islands endlessly exchange momentum, combine, and dismantle.
\textbf{Dynamicity: The Spectrum of the Equalization Engine} \\
On this magnificent trajectory of gathering and scattering, being alive and being dead are never two different worlds. They are merely continuous state points representing differences in 'Dynamicity' on a linear energy scale.
A rock or a piece of iron, which we commonly call 'dead', is simply an entity with extremely low dynamicity; its potential energy is tightly bound, bringing energy exchange with other beings to a near halt. Conversely, the entities we call 'alive' are merely those with high potential energy and extremely strong momentum to promote energy exchange—entities with extremely high dynamicity. Ultimately, the difference between life and non-life is not an essential qualitative difference, but a quantitative difference in the dynamicity (intensity of momentum) that mixes and exchanges the universe's energy.
This profound insight completely overturns biology's outdated attitude toward life. Life is absolutely not an exceptional rebel that miraculously built its own order on the sea of chaos, defying the universe's law of increasing entropy. Rather, life is the most sophisticated 'Local Momentum Structure' formed to hold the most extreme momentum and aggressively flatten (Equalization) the universe's energy gaps faster than anything else.
When a lifeform eats, digests, breathes, and excretes, it fiercely sucks in highly compressed potential energy (like food or sunlight) and explosively scatters massive, chaotic heat and low-quality waste into the environment under the pretext of maintaining its small internal order. Life is not alive because it holds energy still inside; it is alive because it relentlessly exchanges and disperses energy with the outside—accelerating, rather than delaying, the universe's equalization.
\textbf{Redefining Death as Absolute Severance} \\
What, then, is the true nature of 'death' faced by this brilliant life? Death is not simply the stopping of a heartbeat or an end-button erasing an entity from the universe. Death is an event of 'absolute severance' (distance) where the fierce exchange with the larger energy flows is cut off.
While alive, a lifeform was a fierce vortex receiving, mixing, and emitting external energy; but the moment that exchange stops, it degrades into a perfectly isolated energy island. Its internal momentum is exhausted, its dynamicity drops to an extreme low, and it reaches a small 'thermal equilibrium' at the local level.
However, from the macroscopic perspective of the universe, nothing has ended. The matter and energy that composed the dead lifeform are dismantled by decomposers and fiercely reconnected as parts of the soil, atmosphere, and other lifeforms. Death is not a termination, but merely a 'trajectory shift for new connections and gathering/scattering' within the magnificent theater of cosmic equalization.
Ultimately, what fills this universe is not a conflict between the living and the dead. There is only a single, perfect continuum densely packed with lumps of high dynamicity (life) fiercely resolving momentum, and lumps losing that dynamicity (non-life), all while the entire universe endlessly falls toward perfect equalization. We are not strangers thrown onto an empty stage; we are the hottest and most marvelous 'energy vortexes' briefly blooming to fiercely mix and disperse the universe's momentum.
