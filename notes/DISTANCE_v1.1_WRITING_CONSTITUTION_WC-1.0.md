## 0. Purpose and Authority

This document is the runtime control document for writing and revising
the long-form essay **DISTANCE v1.1**.

It exists to prevent longitudinal drift across a manuscript whose
chapters and sections are written over many conversations.

The Constitution is not manuscript prose. Its rules must operate behind
the manuscript and must not normally appear in the essay itself.

For any DISTANCE manuscript-generation or revision request, including a
short request such as `3.5`, `3.5 English`, or `4.2 revise`, the
required workflow is:

**RETRIEVE WC-1.0 → PRE-CHECK → WRITE/REVISE → POST-CHECK → OUTPUT ONLY
THE COMPLIANT MANUSCRIPT**

Memory, conversational recollection, or a prior summary must not
substitute for retrieving the current FROZEN Constitution when the file
is available.

# Part I. Governance

## G-01. Source-of-truth hierarchy

When instructions or manuscript material conflict, apply this priority
order:

1.  The user's latest explicit instruction that explicitly modifies or
    unfreezes a constitutional item.
2.  The current FROZEN DISTANCE v1.1 Writing Constitution.
3.  The current FROZEN Architecture / Canon entries incorporated or
    referenced by this Constitution.
4.  User-approved manuscript text.
5.  User claims and corrections not yet incorporated into the control
    file.
6.  Assistant proposals.
7.  Legacy drafts, including superseded v1.0 material.

An Assistant proposal never becomes Canon merely because it was written
fluently, repeated, or included in a draft.

## G-02. Freeze rule

A FROZEN item may not be silently: - renamed, - reordered, - merged, -
split, - deleted, - generalized, - narrowed, - reinterpreted, - or
replaced.

Only explicit user approval may change a FROZEN item.

## G-03. No implicit constitutional amendment

A local request such as "make this more essay-like," "expand this," or
"rewrite this section" modifies the requested prose only. It does
**not** authorize changes to Canon, Architecture, section boundaries,
terminology, or the v1.1/v2.0 boundary.

## G-04. No compensating drift

Correcting one defect must not create another.

Examples: - Removing meta-writing must not trigger re-teaching
Chapter 1. - Expanding length must not be achieved by importing
later-section material. - Improving readability must not alter Canon. -
Making prose more literary must not weaken conceptual precision. -
Avoiding repetition must not delete a required claim.

## G-05. User approval dominates Assistant inference

If a current draft conflicts with a user-approved correction, the
correction governs even when the draft is newer.

## G-06. Version isolation

DISTANCE v1.1 and later ontology/v2.0 work are separate layers.

**PROHIBITED in v1.1 unless explicitly authorized:** - importing
deferred ontology as established fact; - silently upgrading exploratory
metaphysics into v1.1 Canon; - using v2.0 conclusions to repair gaps in
v1.1; - rewriting v1.1 terminology to fit later ontology.

# Part II. Canonical Core

The following rules are binding conceptual constraints for v1.1
manuscript writing.

## C-01. Canonical causal/dynamical direction

Maintain the direction:

**Difference → Energy Gap → Distance → Momentum → Equalization →
Momentum resolution → effective Distance increase**

Do not reverse this chain merely to make a metaphor sound intuitive.

## C-02. Momentum definition

Canonical sentence:

> **Momentum is the observable directionality associated with unresolved
> Distance.**

Momentum is not to be casually equated with force, velocity, geometric
motion, or a substance that "flows through" a channel.

## C-03. Momentum is always present

Canonical clarification:

> **The existence of Momentum is never the question. Momentum is always
> present. The observable universe is simply the present expression of
> which Momentum becomes effective.**

Do not write as though Momentum first appears only after an object,
force, or local structure has formed.

## C-04. Coexisting Momentum

Canonical sentence:

> **The universe is therefore never composed of independent objects
> acted upon by independent Momentum. It is composed of countless
> coexisting Momentum whose continuously changing balance determines the
> observable directionality of every physical system.**

Do not reduce the universe to independent objects carrying independent
Momentum.

## C-05. Momentum--Distance duality

Maintain:

**larger/stronger effective Momentum ↔ shorter effective Distance**

This is a relation of effectiveness.

## C-06. Resolution direction

Maintain:

**Momentum resolution → increasing effective Distance**

A preferred or strongly available Equalization path must **not** be
described as Equalization causing Distance to shorten.

## C-07. Preferred Equalization path

If a shorter effective/energy Distance corresponds to a more strongly
effective Momentum, the correct direction is:

**shorter effective Distance → stronger effective Momentum → stronger
Equalization tendency / more strongly available resolution path**

Not:

**Equalization → shorter/minimum Distance**

## C-08. Distance is not geometric distance

Effective/relational/energy Distance must not be conflated with
ruler-measured geometric separation.

Geometric convergence can coexist with increasing effective Distance,
and geometric separation does not by itself determine effective
Distance.

## C-09. Energy Gap is not Momentum

Energy Gap and Momentum must remain distinct.

Momentum is directionality associated with unresolved Distance; Energy
Gap must not be casually substituted for Momentum.

## C-10. Potential Momentum

Potential Momentum refers to an energy gap/directionality not presently
expressed as dominant effective observable Momentum under the current
relational configuration.

Do not use Potential Momentum as a synonym for absent Momentum.

## C-11. Equalization terminology

Use **Equalization** as the primary term.

Avoid making "smoothing" the governing term. It may appear sparingly
only when needed for comparison or legacy explanation.

Equalization is not a teleological agent, chooser, optimizer, or
conscious objective.

## C-12. No teleology

Avoid formulations in which: - the universe "wants," - Equalization
"chooses," - nature "tries," - a path is selected after evaluation, - an
Island is intentionally formed.

Preferred paths arise from relational effectiveness, not decision.

## C-13. Island

Use **Island** as the primary DISTANCE term for a relatively persistent
distinguishable configuration.

A physical "structure" may be rendered in Korean according to context as
구조, 구조체, 구성체, 물체, or 아일랜드, but the translation must
preserve whether the text means: - relational configuration, -
persistent physical entity, - or the technical DISTANCE concept Island.

Do not mechanically translate every occurrence of "structure" as "구조."

## C-14. Island formation

Relatively strong Momentum relationships can contribute to binding an
Island, but this must not be written purposefully.

Canonical causal reading:

strong relational bindings coexist with multiple global/local
Equalization processes; they constrain, cancel, and rearrange other
Momentum, producing a relative Momentum lull/persistent configuration
that is observed as an Island/structure.

## C-15. Global/local hierarchy

Maintain the approved hierarchy:

**Global Momentum / global Equalization\
→ relational-network rearrangement\
→ reconstruction of local Difference / Distance / Momentum\
→ local observable dynamics**

Local Momentum is not merely a detached fragment or leftover of Global
Momentum.

Local processes can feed back into the broader configuration, but this
feedback must not erase the explanatory hierarchy.

## C-16. Time

Time is not to be written as an active causal agent.

Avoid prose that makes "time flow" the mechanism producing change.

Where relevant, preserve the v1.1 direction: change is primary; time is
an observational/normalizing scale for change.

## C-17. Space

Do not automatically treat geometric space as the first explanatory
cause of relational change.

Geometric description can remain valid and useful without being silently
promoted to the deepest explanatory layer.

## C-18. Entropy

Entropy is not the governing vocabulary of v1.1.

Use it, if needed, mainly as a comparison to established thermodynamic
language, not as a substitute for Equalization.

## C-19. Universe terminology

Prefer **Universe** over vague appeals to "nature" when discussing the
global DISTANCE framework.

## C-20. Relationships vocabulary

Do not overuse "relationships" as a slogan before the argument has
earned it. Use the term precisely and concretely.

# Part IV. Writing Protocol

## W-01. The manuscript must read as an essay

The reader should encounter the argument, not the author's production
plan.

The Constitution is an internal control mechanism and must remain
invisible in ordinary manuscript prose.

## W-02. Prohibited meta-writing

Remove or avoid routine phrases such as: - "In this chapter, we
will..." - "In this section..." - "The purpose of this section is..." -
"As discussed in Section 3.1..." - "The next section will..." - "We will
leave this to Chapter 4..." - "This prevents overlap with Section
3.6..."

Cross-references may be used only when genuinely useful to the reader,
not to explain manuscript architecture.

## W-03. No chapter-summary scaffolding as a default opening

Do not begin every section by recapping all previous sections.

A short inherited premise may be recalled only when needed to launch the
new argument.

## W-04. No outline-style ending

Do not end a section with a writing-plan preview.

Transition by completing the current argument so that the next question
arises naturally from the prose.

## W-05. Irreversible argumentative progression

The default progression must be:

**A → B → C → D**

not:

**A → A+B → A+B+C → A+B+C+D**

Previously established concepts are premises, not recurring lecture
material.

## W-06. No re-teaching foundational Canon

After Chapter 1, do not repeatedly explain: - what Difference is, - what
Distance is, - what Momentum is, - the Fake Desktop, - why time/space
may be interfaces, - why geometric and effective Distance differ,

unless a later argument genuinely requires a short, targeted reminder.

## W-07. Section payload discipline

Every section must have one primary new argumentative payload.

Expansion should deepen that payload through: - consequences, -
examples, - counter-intuitions, - carefully bounded thought
experiments, - implications, - and conceptual distinctions.

Expansion must not be achieved by importing unrelated Canon or future
sections.

## W-08. Length is not a license to repeat

A target of roughly long-form section length does not authorize: -
restating definitions, - repeating earlier examples, - multiplying
near-synonymous claims, - inserting textbook summaries, - or previewing
later material.

Depth must replace repetition.

## W-09. Subheadings

Use subheadings sparingly.

They should mark major conceptual turns, not every two or three
paragraphs.

The default feel is continuous long-form essay prose, not a report,
lecture note, FAQ, or listicle.

## W-10. Rhetorical questions

Use rhetorical questions only when they genuinely move the argument
forward.

Do not construct an entire section as repeated "Does X mean Y?" mini-FAQ
blocks.

## W-11. Technical precision inside literary prose

Essay style does not authorize conceptual looseness.

Metaphors must remain subordinate to Canon.

If a metaphor reverses the causal direction, remove or rewrite the
metaphor.

## W-12. Scientific-status discipline

DISTANCE v1.1 is an interpretive/conceptual framework unless a specific
quantitative claim has been established.

Do not write speculative correspondence as though it were experimentally
verified physical law.

Distinguish: - observation, - established scientific description, -
DISTANCE reinterpretation, - conjectural extension.

## W-13. Existing science is not a straw man

Do not claim that standard physics "simply says" something it does not.

A successful formalism may be described as a powerful observational
representation even when DISTANCE questions its explanatory priority.

## W-14. Korean/English parity

The Korean and English versions must preserve: - the same claims, - the
same causal direction, - the same scope, - the same degree of
certainty, - the same section boundary.

English editing must not introduce stronger ontology or stronger
scientific claims than the Korean source, and vice versa.

## W-15. Terminology stability

Do not casually rotate among synonyms for key technical terms.

Primary technical terms: **Difference, Energy Gap, Distance, Momentum,
Equalization, Island, Dynamicity**.

When a translation choice is context-sensitive, preserve the technical
distinction rather than literal word-for-word symmetry.

## W-16. Tone

Preferred tone: - serious long-form essay; - conceptually exact; -
readable to an educated non-specialist; - philosophically exploratory
without mystical inflation; - scientifically respectful without
surrendering the framework's critical questions.

Avoid: - sensationalism, - grandiose certainty, - repeated "profound,"
"magnificent," "chilling," etc., - lecture-note diction, - production
notes, - self-congratulation about the theory.

# Part V. Mandatory Runtime Checks

## P-01. PRE-CHECK --- before writing any section

Before generating prose, retrieve the current Constitution and identify:

1.  **Target:** chapter, section, language, revision status.
2.  **Inherited Canon:** what the reader already knows and must not be
    re-taught.
3.  **Section Payload:** the one primary argumentative advance.
4.  **Do Not Repeat:** prior chapter/section material prohibited from
    expansion here.
5.  **Do Not Anticipate:** later material prohibited from detailed
    treatment here.
6.  **Terminology constraints.**
7.  **v1.1/v2.0 boundary.**
8.  **Approved manuscript dependencies**, if any.

If the exact frozen architecture needed for the target section is
missing from the Constitution, retrieve the authoritative architecture
source before writing. Do not invent it.

## P-02. POST-CHECK --- after drafting, before output

The draft must pass all checks below.

### PC-1 Canon compliance

Does any sentence reverse or distort a canonical relation?

### PC-2 Architecture compliance

Does the section stay inside its frozen responsibility?

### PC-3 Prior-chapter duplication

Is Chapter 1 or Chapter 2 being re-taught unnecessarily?

### PC-4 Prior-section duplication

Does the draft substantially re-explain the previous section instead of
advancing?

### PC-5 Forward intrusion

Does the draft steal a later section's central payload?

### PC-6 Global/local hierarchy

Where applicable, has local structure displaced or obscured the broader
Global Momentum / Equalization process?

### PC-7 Distance-direction check

Has "preferred Equalization" accidentally been written as Equalization
causing shorter/minimum Distance?

### PC-8 Geometric/effective Distance check

Has geometric inward/outward, near/far, convergence/divergence been
equated with effective Distance without justification?

### PC-9 Teleology check

Has the universe, Equalization, Momentum, or an Island been given
intention, choice, or optimization agency?

### PC-10 v2.0 contamination check

Has deferred ontology entered v1.1 as established content?

### PC-11 Meta-writing check

Does the prose explain the chapter plan, section boundaries, or future
writing?

### PC-12 Essay-style check

Does it read as continuous essay prose rather than a report, FAQ,
outline, or lecture note?

### PC-13 Repetition/length check

Was length achieved by repetition rather than argumentative depth?

### PC-14 Scientific-status check

Are interpretation, hypothesis, and established scientific description
properly distinguished?

### PC-15 Korean/English parity check

For translations/revisions, did claim strength or meaning drift between
languages?

If any check fails, revise before presenting the manuscript.

## P-03. Completion rule

A section is **not complete when drafted**.

It is complete only when:

**PRE-CHECK PASSED + MANUSCRIPT WRITTEN + POST-CHECK PASSED**

# Part VI. Drift Detection and Recovery

## D-01. Drift indicators

Treat the following as automatic warning signs: - section count
changes; - a frozen title disappears; - 4.14 disappears; - a section
begins re-defining Chapter 1 concepts; - every section starts with a
recap; - every section ends with "the next section..."; - many
micro-subheadings appear; - global Momentum disappears behind local
structures; - Equalization is described as minimizing Distance; -
geometric inward/outward is mapped directly to effective Distance; -
v2.0 ontology appears; - a legacy v1.0 claim overrides a v1.1
correction.

## D-02. Recovery rule

When drift is detected: 1. do not patch only the offending sentence if
the argument has structurally drifted; 2. return to the target Section
Payload; 3. remove imported material; 4. restore the canonical causal
direction; 5. rewrite only the affected span; 6. rerun the complete
POST-CHECK.

## D-03. No silent repair of the Constitution

If the manuscript appears to require a constitutional change, stop
treating that change as ordinary editing.

Mark it as a proposed constitutional amendment and require explicit user
approval.
